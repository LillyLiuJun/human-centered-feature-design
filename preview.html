Core Purpose & Usage Scenarios

Human-Centered Feature Design is a reasoning framework designed to test and refine features to ensure they genuinely fulfill authentic user needs.

It deduces what users actually require in specific situations—from macro feature definitions down to micro interaction details—grounding product solutions in human psychology rather than team-centric vanity or synthetic stickiness.

Production Retrospectives

Two grounded case studies demonstrating theoretical deduction resolving real commercial UX problems:

Case 01: Topic Card System — Content Contract & Context Alignment
Context
AI language immersion app. Daily topic discovery cards had poor engagement.
Diagnosis
Users during dedicated practice already have high motivation; the true bottleneck was decision friction (broad categories felt like unquantifiable homework), breaking the contract between card promise and screen delivery.
Refactor
Shifted from categories to strict contract cards ("Talk about yesterday's dream in 3 min"); managed digestive debt instead of static inventory.

Case 02: Dynamic Reading States — The 3 Situational Boundaries
Context
Long-form reading card with tap-to-expand state.
Diagnosis
Card expansion is an ephemeral "transient attentional state," not permanent content. Persisting it leaves cognitive clutter upon return; mechanical auto-collapse cuts off reading flow.
Refactor
Established 3 strict situational boundaries: protect flow during micro-interruptions in viewport; silently reset when fully scrolled off-screen; silent reset upon leaving the module.

The Foundational Worldview

All situational deductions stem from three immutable assumptions regarding human psychology:

01 · HOMEOSTASIS
Human Homeostasis
Humans are self-regulating systems. Need stems from disequilibrium. Products cannot invent desire; they only serve as relief valves.
02 · AGENCY
Sense of Agency
Humans are cognitively fragile yet crave sovereignty. The system scaffolds invisibly and credits the human. The best system is quiet.
03 · CONTEXT
Situated Context
Humans live in concrete situations. Influence is borrowed from contextual fit, never permanently owned by the product.

The Three Litmus Tests

Before any feature ships, audit it ruthlessly against these three filters:

  • Does this return the human to equilibrium, or manufacture synthetic anxiety?
  • Does the user feel sovereign agency, or are they being piloted by system mechanics?
  • Does this embed into the user's authentic situation, or create tone-deaf noise?
